    Your rules of the road

    How this works and our assumptions
    • Liability matching: Safety holds the present value of the next 1–4 years of income, discounted at its own return. Income holds the years after that. Everything else goes into Growth.
    • Required return is the single monthly rate at which your corpus exactly funds your rising income until the end of the plan (plus any legacy). It is before tax and fees. The simulations include both.
    • Market scenarios: yearly returns are drawn at random using each bucket's return and volatility, with equity and bonds lightly correlated. The same scenarios are used for every split, so the comparison is fair.
    • Refills: checked every month. After good markets, equity is sold to refill Safety. After bad markets, Income pays instead and equity is left alone. Each year, Growth is rebalanced against Income: trimmed after rallies, and topped up after falls only while Income still holds 2 years of spending.
    • Tax: new regime, FY 2025-26. FD and liquid-fund interest and debt gains are taxed at your slab rate. Equity gains above ₹1.25 L a year are taxed at 12.5%, and the tax-free gains are harvested every year.
    • Returns are assumptions, not guarantees. This is a planning aid, not SEBI-registered investment advice.
    Profile & horizon

    The horizon runs until the later of the two plan-until ages. Women in India outlive men by about 3 years on average, so plan for the survivor.

    Current corpus & allocation

    Return is the long-run CAGR you expect, after which the fee is deducted. Volatility drives the Monte Carlo (Nifty about 18%, debt funds 2–4%, FD 0). Cost % is the share of today's value that is your original investment, so only gains are taxed. Bucket is used only by the bucket strategy. Locked till covers lock-ins such as PPF maturity or tax-saver FDs. Leave out your own home and the emergency fund.

    Spending in retirement

    Spending starts at retirement and steps up with inflation once a year. Medical costs and premiums usually rise much faster than general inflation, and premiums jump at age bands such as 61+ and 71+.

    Spending phases (go-go → slow-go → no-go)

    Travel and lifestyle spending usually falls in your 70s and 80s, while medical spending keeps rising. The phases apply only to living expenses.

    Saving until retirement

    New money is split according to your target allocation. Add EPF maturity, gratuity, leave encashment, the NPS 60% lump sum or a property sale as inflows under Goals & one-off cash flows.

    Other income in retirement

    Enter pension, annuity, rent or consulting income in today's ₹. Each one grows at its own rate from today: use 0% for a fixed annuity (and enter its actual payout) and about 4–5% for rent. Payments continue until the year before "Until age".

    Goals & one-off cash flows

    Examples: children's education or wedding, home renovation, replacing the car every 8–10 years, a big trip, support for parents, or an inheritance (inflow). Amounts are in today's ₹ and grow at their own inflation rate.

    Withdrawal strategy

    Spending comes from bucket 1. Each year bucket 1 is refilled to its target, from equity (bucket 3) in a positive year and from bucket 2 otherwise. Bucket 2 is topped up from equity only in positive years.

    Tax (FY 2025-26 rules, editable)

    Not modelled: surcharge (income above ₹50 L), STCG (holdings are assumed to be over 12 months), and loss carry-forward. FD interest is taxed every year as it accrues, while debt-fund gains are taxed at slab rate only when sold. The 87A rebate does not reduce tax on LTCG.

    Simulation

    Monte Carlo draws a random return for every asset each year from its volatility. This shows sequence-of-returns risk: a crash early in retirement does far more damage than the same crash later.

      Checklist: what people forget before retiring

      Health

      • Buy your own health cover before 55. Employer cover ends when you retire, and new policies carry 2–4 year waiting periods for pre-existing conditions.
      • Add a super top-up (₹25 L–₹1 Cr) on top of the base floater. It is cheap, and one hospitalisation can cost ₹10 L or more.
      • Plan for things insurance does not cover: OPD visits, medicines, dental, home nursing and long-term care.
      • Sign a living will / advance directive (legally valid in India since 2018) and a healthcare power of attorney.

      Income & products

      • SCSS: ₹30 L limit per person at 8.2%. A couple can invest ₹60 L.
      • NPS at 60: up to 60% as a tax-free lump sum, and at least 40% must buy an annuity, which is taxable and not inflation-linked.
      • EPF stops earning interest once the account turns inoperative after retirement, so plan when to withdraw or move it.
      • Gratuity is tax-free up to ₹20 L and leave encashment up to ₹25 L (non-government employees).
      • Ladder your FDs and bonds so they do not all mature, and get re-invested, when rates are low.
      • Avoid locking money in ULIPs, endowment plans or long annuities just before retirement.

      Tax & paperwork

      • Submit Form 15H to banks if your total income is below the taxable limit, so TDS is not deducted.
      • At 75+, with only pension and interest from the same bank, you can skip filing an ITR (Sec 194P). Otherwise, keep filing.
      • Use the yearly ₹1.25 L equity LTCG exemption, and time debt-fund redemptions across financial years.
      • Consolidate bank, demat and MF accounts. Update KYC and mobile numbers so you do not lose access.

      Family & estate

      • Write a will, and add nominations on every bank account, MF folio, demat, EPF, NPS and insurance policy.
      • Hold accounts jointly as "either or survivor", and make sure your spouse knows every account and login.
      • Agree in advance how much support you will give adult children and ageing parents. It is a common hidden drain.
      • Term insurance is rarely needed after retirement if nobody depends on your income.

      Home, lifestyle & safety

      • Clear home loans before retiring. Budget for maintenance, society charges, property tax and making the home safe for old age.
      • Your house is not part of the corpus unless you plan to sell it, downsize or take a reverse mortgage.
      • The first 2–3 years of retirement are often the most expensive (travel, hobbies).
      • Fraud targeting retirees ("digital arrest", KYC scams) is rising. Keep only a small balance in the account linked to UPI.
      • Review this plan every year: update actual spending and returns, then rebalance.
